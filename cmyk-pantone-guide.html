How to find your Pantone match

  1. Start from your HEX code. Note your brand's HEX, RGB and the CMYK value above.
  2. Use an official reference. Look up the closest colors in Pantone Connect or a physical Pantone Formula Guide (coated and uncoated versions look different).
  3. Compare in good light. Screens aren't reliable. Check printed swatches in daylight next to your printed proof.
  4. Pick coated (C) and uncoated (U). Glossy and matte papers absorb ink differently, so record both in your brand guidelines.
  5. Confirm with your printer. Ask for a proof before printing large quantities.

Tips for print-ready brand colors

  • Avoid ultra-bright "neon" screen colors for print — they usually can't be reproduced in CMYK.
  • For rich black text, use K 100%. For large black areas, ask your printer for their recommended rich black.
  • Keep a color table in your brand guidelines with HEX, RGB, CMYK and Pantone for every brand color.
  • Send print files as PDF with fonts outlined or embedded, in CMYK, with 3mm bleed.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert HEX to CMYK?

Enter your HEX code above and the CMYK percentages appear instantly. The formula divides each RGB value by 255, works out the black (K) amount, then calculates cyan, magenta and yellow from what's left.

Why does my color look different when printed?

Screens make color with light (RGB) and can show brighter colors than ink can print (CMYK). Very bright blues, greens and oranges often look duller on paper. Paper type, coating and the printer's profile change the result too, so always ask for a proof.

What's the difference between CMYK and Pantone?

CMYK prints colors by mixing four inks in tiny dots. Pantone (a spot color system) uses a pre-mixed ink for one exact color, so it stays consistent across printers and materials. Pantone costs more but is the safest choice for logos and packaging.

How do I find the Pantone color for my HEX code?

Use Pantone's official tools — Pantone Connect or a printed Pantone Formula Guide — and compare under good light. Pick the closest swatch by eye, then ask your printer to confirm. Online HEX-to-Pantone matches are only approximate.

Do I need Pantone colors for my brand?

Not always. Digital-only brands can live with HEX and RGB. If you print signage, packaging, uniforms or large runs of stationery, adding a Pantone reference to your brand guidelines keeps your colors consistent.

Which CMYK value should I give my printer?

Use the values here as a starting point, then ask your printer which color profile they use (for example Coated FOGRA39 in the UAE and Europe) and request a printed proof before the full run.
