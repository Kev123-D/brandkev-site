Guide

How to pair fonts for your brand

  1. Start with the mood. Serif fonts feel classic, trusted or luxurious. Sans-serif fonts feel modern, clean and friendly. Rounded fonts feel playful.
  2. Give each font one job. Use the expressive font for headlines and the calm one for paragraphs, buttons and menus.
  3. Create contrast. Pair a serif with a sans-serif, or a heavy display font with a light body font, so the difference looks intentional.
  4. Test real content. Type your actual headline and a real sentence above. Check it on your phone, too.
  5. Plan for Arabic early. If you'll publish in Arabic, choose a pair that covers both scripts so your brand looks consistent in both languages.
FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What makes a good font pairing?

Contrast with harmony. Pair fonts that are clearly different in role — like a characterful heading font with a calm, readable body font — but share a similar mood and proportions. Most brands need only two fonts.

Are these fonts free for commercial use?

Yes. Every font here is from Google Fonts and released under open licenses (mostly the SIL Open Font License), so you can use them in logos, websites, print and ads for free.

Which fonts support Arabic?

The Arabic + English pairs — Cairo with Tajawal, and Readex Pro with IBM Plex Sans Arabic — include both Arabic and Latin characters, so your English and Arabic text match. That's ideal for bilingual brands in the UAE and the Gulf.

How do I add these fonts to my website?

Click Embed code and paste it into the <head> of your site, then click Copy CSS and paste it into your stylesheet. In Canva or Figma, simply search for the font names.

How many fonts should a brand use?

Two is the sweet spot: one for headings and one for body text. A third font (often a monospace or script) can work for small accents, but more than that usually looks messy.
